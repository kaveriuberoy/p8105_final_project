Violence and Covid

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic reshaped nearly every aspect of public health. Yet one of the most concerning and less discussed trends was the simultaneous rise in community violence. According to the Center for American Progress, gun violence surged during the pandemic, with structural inequities and community-level vulnerabilities playing a major role. You can read more about this in their article: “COVID-19’s Impact on Gun Violence in America”.

Motivated by this national pattern, this analysis explores whether homicide rates, fatal police shootings, and overall violent crime rates were related to COVID-19 cases and deaths at the U.S. county level. By combining county-level violence indicators with pandemic outcomes, we aim to understand whether communities already burdened by structural violence experienced disproportionately severe effects during COVID-19.

Visualizations

This plot highlights the 15 counties with the highest fatal police shooting rates between 2017 and 2020. The bar height shows shootings per 100,000 residents, and the fill color shows COVID-19 deaths per 100,000 in 2020. It displays whether places with high exposure to police violence also experienced high COVID mortality. A horizontal bar chart with a color gradient was chosen for this display because it makes it easy to rank counties while simultaneously visualizing COVID burden as a second dimension.

This scatterplot illustrates the relationship between fatal police shootings per 100,000 residents (summed from 2017 to 2020) and COVID-19 deaths per 100,000 in 2020 across U.S. counties. The points represent individual counties, and the fitted linear trend line shows that there is no strong association between fatal police shootings and COVID-19 mortality. Most counties cluster near zero fatal shootings, and the regression line is nearly flat, suggesting that the level of police violence in a county did not meaningfully predict COVID-19 death rates. A scatterplot with a fitted regression line was chosen because it is the most direct way to assess the shape, strength, and linearity of the relationship between two continuous variables before fitting formal models.

Model 1: This scatterplot explores whether counties with more fatal police shootings also experienced higher COVID-19 mortality in 2020. Most counties have zero or very few fatal police shootings per 100k, which creates heavy clustering on the left side of the graph. The regression line is flat, indicating little to no linear relationship between fatal police shootings and COVID-19 death rates. While these forms of structural violence may be connected through deeper social factors, this crude model does not show a strong direct association. A simple unadjusted regression was create to quantify the crude association first, so that any added value of later, more complex models could be interpreted relative to this baseline.

Model 2: This plot examines whether violent crime rates are associated with COVID-19 mortality at the county level. Here, the regression line slopes upward, suggesting that counties with higher violent crime rates tend to experience higher COVID-19 deaths per 100k. Although the relationship is still noisy, the positive trend indicates that broader community violence may be linked with higher pandemic vulnerability. Violent crime rate was chosen for this regression because it is a more continuous and less sparse measure than fatal shootings, making it better suited for detecting graded, population-level associations with COVID-19 mortality.

Model 3: This figure shows how predicted COVID-19 deaths per 100,000 change as each violence indicator increases, holding the other two measures at their median values. The line for homicides per 100k rises steeply, indicating that higher homicide rates are strongly associated with higher COVID-19 mortality. The line for violent crime rate slopes upward more gently, suggesting a weaker but still positive association. In contrast, the line for fatal police shootings per 100k is nearly flat, showing that fatal police shootings are not a strong predictor of COVID-19 deaths once overall violence levels are taken into account.A multivariable regression with marginal effects plots were created in order to isolate the independent relationship of each violence indicator with COVID-19 deaths while holding the others constant. Later, the coefficients were translated into visual predictions over realistic ranges of each predictor.
